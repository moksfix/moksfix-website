If you are planning a mobile app, the first question is almost always the same: how much will it cost? The honest answer is that mobile app development cost in India ranges widely, because an app's price follows its features, not a fixed menu. This guide gives you realistic rupee ranges for 2026, explains what actually drives the number, and shows how to keep the cost sensible without cutting corners.

## What a mobile app costs in India: typical ranges

Below are realistic 2026 ranges for the Indian market. Treat them as scope-dependent bands, not fixed prices. A clear brief with fewer features lands near the lower end; integrations, custom design and complex logic push you higher.

| App type | What it includes | Typical cost (INR) |
|---|---|---|
| Simple / informational app | Static content, basic screens, contact forms, no login | ₹1,50,000 - ₹4,00,000 |
| Standard app with backend + accounts | User login, a database, profiles, notifications, an admin panel | ₹4,00,000 - ₹12,00,000 |
| Marketplace / on-demand app | Multiple user roles, payments, live tracking, ratings, matching | ₹12,00,000 - ₹30,00,000 |
| Complex app | Heavy integrations, real-time data, custom algorithms, high scale | ₹25,00,000+ |

These ranges assume senior people doing the build, proper testing, and a working app you actually own, not a throwaway prototype.

## What drives the cost of an app

Two apps that look similar on the surface can differ hugely in price. Here is what moves the number.

- **Features and screens.** Every screen, workflow and rule takes design, build and testing time. A login, a search, a payment and a chat are four separate pieces of work, not one.
- **Platforms.** Supporting both iOS and Android costs more than one. The gap is smaller with a shared codebase, but testing and polish still double across two sets of devices.
- **Backend.** If the app stores data, has user accounts or needs an admin panel, it needs a server behind it. That backend is often a meaningful share of the budget, and it is essentially its own piece of software.
- **Design.** A clean, standard design is quicker. Custom animations, a distinctive brand look and lots of unique screens add time.
- **Integrations.** Payments, maps, SMS, WhatsApp, CRMs and ERPs each add work. Third-party services also carry their own ongoing fees.
- **Non-functional needs.** Scale, security and compliance requirements quietly add engineering effort that users never see but absolutely pay for.

### Native vs cross-platform, briefly

Fully native means a separate app for iOS and a separate app for Android, each written in that platform's own language. It gives the best performance and the fullest access to device hardware, but you are effectively funding two builds.

Cross-platform uses one codebase for both stores. For most business apps this is the pragmatic choice: it lowers both the build cost and the long-term maintenance cost, because one team maintains one codebase. ## One-time vs ongoing costs

The build is a one-time cost. An app is a living product, so plan for the running costs too, or the launch budget will surprise you later.

**One-time costs**

- Design and development of the app itself
- The backend and admin panel, if the app needs one
- Initial setup of hosting and third-party accounts

**Ongoing costs**

- **App store fees.** Apple charges a yearly developer fee of roughly ₹8,000 to ₹10,000. Google charges a one-time registration fee of about ₹2,000.
- **Hosting and services.** The backend needs a server, and paid integrations bill monthly based on usage. For a small app this can start modestly and grow with your user base.
- **Maintenance and updates.** Phones and operating systems change every year. Without regular updates, an app slowly breaks. A realistic yearly maintenance budget is around 15 to 20 percent of the original build cost, covering OS updates, fixes and small improvements.

If your app leans heavily on a web dashboard or complex server logic, that [backend is built as a web application](/services/web-application-development) and is worth scoping clearly from the start, since it carries both build and running costs.

## How to keep app costs down with an MVP-first approach

The biggest cost mistake is trying to build everything in version one. The fix is an MVP, a minimum viable product that does the one or two things your users genuinely need, launched quickly so you can learn from real usage.

- **Rank features ruthlessly.** Separate the must-haves from the nice-to-haves. Nice-to-haves wait for version two, funded by what you learn.
- **Launch on one platform first.** If your audience leans Android, start there, prove the idea, then expand. You halve the initial build.
- **Reuse proven components.** Standard patterns for login, payments and notifications are faster and cheaper than reinventing them.
- **Build in weekly increments.** You see working software early and can adjust before money is spent on the wrong thing. That is how we work, with senior people building and no junior hand-offs.
- **Get a fixed-scope quote.** Vague scope leads to vague bills. A transparent, fixed-scope quote tells you the number before work starts, so there are no surprises halfway through.

Done well, an MVP typically ships in 6 to 12 weeks and costs a fraction of an all-in-one-go build, while giving you a real app in users' hands much sooner.
